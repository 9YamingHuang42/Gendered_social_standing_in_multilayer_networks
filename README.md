# Gendered_social_standing_in_multilayer_networks
This study examined gender differences in social standing across everyday social and reputational networks in 14 Tibetan villages. Everyday social networks were further divided into three layers, consanguineal kin, affinal kin, and friends. Reputational networks were further divided into four layers, generosity, religosity, informal leadership, and knowledge. We adopted a multidimensional and multilayer network framework to distinguishe three aspects of social standing (received recognition, structural position, and multiplex position across everyday social and reputational networks) and examined revelant gender differences guided by the hypothesized causal framework. For details, received recognition was measured using in-degree centrality and PageRank. Structural position was measured using betweenness centrality, inward harmonic centrality, triangle count, and community size. Multiplex position was measured using cross-layer recognition and cross-layer ties, capturing whether individuals were nominated across networks and whether individuals were nominated by the same nominees across networks, respectively. We further examined how these gender differences vary with age, post-marital residence, kinship systems, and market participation using Bayesian multilevel models. We also examined direct-effect analyses used additional DAG-guided covariate adjustments, as supplementary analyses to see the robustness of total effects.
